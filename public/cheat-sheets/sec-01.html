The CIA triad

Every security control serves one or more of these three goals.

GoalMeaningBroken byProtect with
ConfidentialityOnly authorized people can see the dataData leak, stolen passwordEncryption, access control
IntegrityData stays accurate and unalteredTampering, malwareHashes, signatures
AvailabilitySystems and data work when neededRansomware, DDoS, outageBackups, redundancy, patching

AAA: who, what, did what

TermMeaning
AuthenticationProve who you are
AuthorizationWhat you may do once in
AccountingLog what you did (audit trail)

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) uses two or more factor types: something you know (password), something you have (app, key) and something you are (fingerprint).

Risk in plain words

TermMeaning
AssetAnything worth protecting
ThreatSomething that could cause harm
VulnerabilityA weakness; an exploit uses it

Risk = likelihood × impact.

Control types

Categories: technical, managerial, operational and physical.

TypeDoes whatExample
PreventiveStops it happeningFirewall, MFA
DeterrentDiscourages attackersWarning banner, cameras
DetectiveSpots it happeningIDS, log review
CorrectiveFixes the damageRestore from backup
CompensatingStand-in when you can't use the usual controlExtra logging
DirectiveTells people what to doSecurity policy

Design principles

PrincipleMeaning
Least privilegeOnly the access the job needs
Defense in depthLayers, so one failure is not fatal
Zero trustNo trust from network location alone
Separation of dutiesNo one controls a whole process
Non-repudiationCan't deny an action (signatures, logs)

NIST CSF 2.0: six functions

FunctionMeaning
GovernStrategy, policy and roles
IdentifyKnow your assets and risks
ProtectSafeguards that reduce risk
DetectFind attacks and compromises
RespondAct on an incident
RecoverRestore operations

Key terms

TermMeaning
CVEPublic ID for a known vulnerability
Zero-dayExploited before a patch exists
IOCEvidence of compromise (IP, hash)
IDS / IPSDetects / detects and blocks
SIEMCollects and correlates logs
EDREndpoint monitoring and response

Stay safe and legal

Danger. Suspect a breach? Disconnect, do not wipe, and escalate to security.

Authorized use only. Test only what you own or have written permission for.

Caution. Never share MFA codes with anyone.

Remember

  • C-I-A: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability
  • Authentication is who; authorization is what
  • No control is perfect: use layers
